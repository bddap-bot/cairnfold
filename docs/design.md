# Cairnfold design

Cairnfold is a cozy building toy for VR, inspired by Tiny Glade: no goals, no
failure, just a meadow and tools that turn rough strokes into stone walls, paths,
houses and gardens that look hand-made. You draw in the space around you, stand in
what you built at life size, or teleport into the air and float above it like a
diorama.

It is a clean-room game. Every asset is generated by code in this repository or
authored for it; nothing comes from Tiny Glade's files, and nothing here is derived
from inspecting them. The "how the original does it" column below summarises what
its developers have said publicly (sources at the end), so we know what feel we are
aiming at; our implementation is our own.

## Requirements

- Standalone on the Steam Frame, launched from a Steam library shortcut.
- Hold the headset's refresh rate at all times. A frame that misses vsync in VR is
  felt, not seen; every stage is measured on the device before it lands.
- Original assets only, generated procedurally wherever possible.
- One input mapping that works on any OpenXR controller with a stick, trigger,
  grip and two face buttons.

## Engine and target

**Decision: Godot 4.5 (Mobile renderer, OpenXR), running standalone on the Frame.**

Two options were considered:

| Option | What it costs | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Standalone on the Frame (Snapdragon-class SoC, Adreno GPU) | A mobile GPU budget: forward/tiled rendering, no screen-space GI, careful overdraw | Measured: see "Frame measurements" below. Godot's official aarch64 Linux build runs on the Frame's SteamOS and drives SteamVR's OpenXR runtime directly |
| B. Rendered on a desktop PC, streamed to the Frame | A desktop GPU budget, but every frame crosses Wi-Fi: encode, transmit, decode, reproject | Desktop-to-Frame streaming sessions measured on our setup show inconsistent delivered frame rates and stalls; that path is being reworked separately. A game that must hold refresh cannot depend on it today |

Standalone wins on the requirement that matters most, a steady refresh rate, and
it removes a PC from the loop. The look is designed for the mobile budget
(below). If streaming becomes reliable, the same Godot project can switch to the
Forward+ renderer on a PC with no code change, which would unlock heavier lighting
as an optional tier.

Godot rather than a custom Rust/Vulkan engine: OpenXR, multiview stereo,
instancing, shadows and fog come built in, the official binary runs on aarch64
Linux, and GDScript reloads without a compile step. Generators are plain data code
(`WallLayout` builds stone transforms, nodes only render them), so a hot generator
can move to a GDExtension later without touching the rest.

### Frame measurements

Every session writes `log/metrics.json` in the install: per second, frame-time
percentiles, frames over 1.5× the refresh period, and `gpu_ms`, the GPU time the
game process used in that second (summed `drm-engine-*` time from the kernel's
fdinfo, so it is real on the device whether or not the runtime reports any).
`info` records the render-target size, the render scale and each eye's field of
view.

With the headset off the head the runtime does not render the game's frames: the
game's GPU time is about 0.3 ms per frame and every frame lands on vsync. Runs taken
that way say nothing about headroom, and the table below is one of them. A run with
the headset worn (Godot 4.5.1 Mobile renderer, Turnip Adreno 750, SteamVR 2.18.2,
1728×1728 per eye, 4× MSAA, one drawn wall, before the flora stage) rendered at
27.78 ms per frame, every other 72 Hz refresh, with about 16 ms of GPU time per
frame against a 13.89 ms budget. Holding the refresh rate is open work; measure it
from worn sessions' `metrics.json`.

The runtime's recommended render target is 1728×1728 per eye on a 2160×2160 panel,
and Valve's foveation layer (`VK_LAYER_VALVE_fdm_injection`) lowers shading density
outside the gaze in every frame. `--render-scale` sets the render target relative
to the runtime's recommendation.

Earlier run, headset off the head, `demo/headset.json`:

| Phase | Seconds | Frame time p99 | Frames over 1.5× the refresh period |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meadow idle | 19 | 13.89 ms | 3, all in the first second (session start) |
| Drawing a wall (live rebuild) | 19 | 13.89 ms | 0 |
| Floating 6 m up | 67 | 16.67 ms | 2, both at the moment of the air teleport (~142 ms) |

## Look on a mobile budget

The original's look rests on soft global illumination, warm sun, painterly
colour and dense, touchable detail. On a mobile GPU in stereo at the headset's refresh rate we get it by:

- **Light:** one warm directional sun with soft two-split shadows; sky ambient
  from a hand-tuned sky shader (zenith-to-horizon gradient, sun glow, painted noise
  clouds); baked per-instance occlusion (lower courses darker, wall bases darker)
  standing in for GI darkening; wrap-lambert diffuse for soft terminators; foliage
  backlight for the glowing-leaf look.
- **Atmosphere:** depth fog with aerial perspective and sun scatter, tinted towards
  the sky, so distant hills fade blue and the near meadow stays saturated.
- **Grading:** AgX tonemapping, mild saturation and contrast lift, gentle glow.
- **Detail:** everything repeated is a MultiMesh: stones, grass clumps, flowers,
  foliage. Stones are rounded, lumpy boxes with per-stone colour, so walls read as
  individual pieces. Grass is geometry (no alpha test) to stay cheap on tiled GPUs.

## Element inventory

Stage numbers refer to the build order at the end.

| Element | How the original does it | Our VR approach | Stage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Freehand walls | Drag on the ground; gridless, follows terrain, stroke speed sets corner sharpness; bricks laid by GPU compute | Hold trigger and sweep the pointer over the ground (from the ground or from the air). The stroke is Catmull-Rom smoothed and courses of individual stones are laid live while you draw | 1 (done) |
| Wall height | Height gizmo; walls from very low to very high | Grip a wall and raise or lower your hand; height snaps to whole stone courses, so it grows and shrinks a row at a time | 1 (done) |
| Wall tops | Merlons on flat tops, can be turned off; wooden railings on half-timber | Per wall: capping slabs with merlons, plain capping, or dry-stone coping (stones on edge, tall and short in turn); A on a wall's top cycles them | 1 (done), 4 (done) |
| Arches and gates | "Wall chemistry": a path through a tall wall makes an arch; wide gaps become arcades with pillars; doors widen into archways | Walls of 5 m or more open an arch at the middle; A on a wall cuts or fills an arch there. Stage 5 adds the chemistry: a path stroke through a wall cuts the arch, wide crossings become arcades | 1 (done), 5 |
| Wall ends and corners | Neater cornerstones; towers by drawing a loop | Stone piers at wall ends and at the corners of a house | 1 (done), 4 (done) |
| Paths | Brush-painted sand, stones, cobbles; stones arrange along the path; stairs on rock | Trigger-sweep with a path brush; stones laid along the stroke and pressed into the terrain shader's mask so grass parts around them | 5 |
| Towers | A loop held down straightens into a circle | Draw a loop and keep holding with the end back near the start: after a moment it snaps to a round tower (radius 1.2–4 m) with a tiled cone roof, standing level on its lowest ground | 4 (done) |
| Houses | Rectangle and circle footprints, resizable and rotatable, raised on columns | Two-handed: both grips while pointing at the ground; the two spots are the ends of the ridge, so pulling apart lengthens the house (2.6–14 m, depth following) and twisting turns it. Stone walls with corner piers, a door arch in the front, level on its lowest ground | 4 (done) |
| Roofs | Tile-by-tile roofs; drag to change curvature; flat, peaked, curved, gable | Gable roofs on houses, cone roofs on towers, every tile laid individually as one MultiMesh, plaster gable ends. | Windows, doors | Cottage and gothic families that fuse, adapt and wear the plaster around them; doors where windows meet the ground | Point and click on a wall to place; neighbours fuse; doors at ground level | 6 |
| Chimneys, flags, lanterns | Placed on walls and roofs; lanterns light the fog | Point and click; lanterns as cheap emissive cards plus a few real lights | 6 |
| Fences | Same controls as walls, own tool; a path through a fence makes a wooden arch | Wall drawing with a fence profile | 6 |
| Ivy | Grows over time; finds nearby walls through a spatial hash and climbs between them; avoids arches and windows | Every finished wall grows ivy over about half a minute: vines start at the foot of both faces, wander upward, branch, and steer around arch openings; leaves are instanced cards on the face (`IvyLayout`) | 3 (done) |
| Grass, flowers, bushes | Brush-painted vegetation, layered, painting again makes it bushier | Instanced geometry grass and flowers. Left trigger paints where the left hand points, X switches grass and flowers; the brush widens with distance and painting a spot again thickens it. | Trees | Placed one at a time, random look, billboards that suggest leaves | Procedural trunks and lumpy foliage blobs; left grip plants one where the left hand points | 1 (done), 3 (done) |

| Water | Dug holes with a flat surface and normal-map waves | Dig with a downward pull, flat water with scrolling normals and a depth tint | 7 |
| Global illumination | Software ray-traced GI at quarter resolution, SH-encoded and denoised | Mobile budget: baked per-instance occlusion, sky ambient, a low-res light-probe volume rebuilt when building changes | 2 |

## VR controls

| Action | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Teleport onto the ground | Left stick forward: a straight ray from the left hand lands where it meets the ground; release the stick to go |
| Teleport into the air | Aim above the horizon: with no ground within reach (10 m to start), the ray targets the point in the air at its end, a ring on a pole down to the ground below. Right stick up or down while aiming moves that point farther or nearer (2–40 m). Release to arrive, slightly high, then settle gently into a hover with a slow bob |
| Come back down | Aim at the ground, or press the left D-pad up, left or right to glide down to the ground below |
| Turn | Right stick left or right: 30° snap turn |
| Draw a wall | Right trigger held, sweep the pointer across the ground; release to finish. Strokes under 1 m are discarded |
| Build a tower | Draw a loop and hold the trigger with the end back at the start until it snaps round |
| Raise or lower a wall | Right grip while pointing at a wall, move the hand up or down; the gain grows with distance so far walls are easy to adjust |
| Shape a roof | Right grip while pointing at a roof: up and down for pitch, sideways for curvature |
| Cut or fill an arch | A while pointing at a wall |
| Change a wall's top | A while pointing at its top: merlons, plain capping, coping |
| Undo the last wall | Hold the left D-pad down for half a second; a tap does nothing, and each hold removes one wall |
| Paint flowers or grass | Left trigger held while pointing at the ground; the ring shows the brush, which widens the farther you point |
| Switch flowers and grass | X (B and Y too) |
| Plant a tree | Left grip while pointing at the ground, on release |
| Lay out a house | Both grips while pointing at the ground; pull apart to size, twist to rotate, release to build |
| Later: paint paths | The left-hand brush with a path mode |
| Later: move the sun | Grip the sun in the sky and drag it |

Buttons are named as on the Steam Frame: A, B, X and Y on the right controller,
a D-pad on the left. Godot 4.5 has no Frame interaction profile, so the game binds
the Touch and Index profiles and SteamVR remaps them: B, X and Y arrive as one
button (Touch right B), D-pad down as Touch left X, and the other D-pad directions
as Touch left Y. Whatever B does, X and Y do too, so that button only switches
paint mode; nothing that removes walls sits on a single tap. The table lives in
`Driver.FRAME_BUTTONS`, and `tests/test_controls.gd` presses every button with walls
built.

Floating turns the meadow into a diorama: drawing works from any height because
the pointer ray hits the terrain, so you can lay out a whole castle from above and
then teleport down to stand in its gate.

## Build order

Ordered by what makes it feel like the original soonest. Progress is tracked in
the epic, https://github.com/bddap-bot/cairnfold/issues/1, in this order:

1. First playable: meadow, sky and fog, teleport and float, walls with stones, cap, merlons, piers and arches. (Done.)
   Still open before stage 2:
   - A Steam library shortcut on the Frame launches the game standalone, installed by `scripts/deploy.sh`.
   - The air teleport holds refresh: no frame over 1.5× the refresh period at the moment it happens.
2. Lighting pass: bounce light from a probe volume rebuilt on edits, contact darkening, shadow split and fog tuning for floating viewpoints, compared side by side with the mood target.
3. Ivy and flora: ivy over walls and around openings, flower and grass painting with the left hand, tree placement by pointing. (Done.)
4. Towers, houses and roofs: loop-to-tower, two-handed footprints, tiled roofs with pull-to-shape, wall-top style per wall. (Done.)
5. Paths and wall chemistry: path brush; a path through a wall cuts an arch, wide crossings become arcades.
6. Windows, doors, fences, chimneys, lanterns and flags.
7. Terrain shaping and water.
8. Time of day, seasons and palettes, a colour picker.
9. Sound and music. (Done.)
10. Save and load, undo history and the eraser.
11. Wildlife and photo mode.

## Sound

Every sound is synthesised by `scripts/compose.py` into `game/audio/`; rerun it
(`nix-shell --run 'python3 scripts/compose.py'`) after changing it, never edit the
files by hand. The runtime only plays them: Godot mixes on its audio thread, so
sound costs no frame time.

- Music: two short motifs (a B-major call with a pickup into each long note, and a
  B-minor arpeggio that settles into a D–C# eighth-note ostinato) worked into twelve
  sections: plain statements, reharmonisations, a waltz, a canon, a mirror
  inversion, fragments traded between voices, the relative major, dorian colour, and
  both motifs overlaid in minor and in major, at 72–138 bpm. Each section has three
  synchronized stems: bed (pad and bass), lead and ornament. They loop as one
  playlist from a random start, crossfading at section ends.
- Building thins the music: while a wall is being drawn or pulled or flora is
  painted, and for a few seconds after any edit, the ornament fades out and the
  lead drops 14 dB; the bed carries on. The tune returns gradually once you stop.
- Comfort in a headset: soft felt plucks, a low breathy flute, mallets and pads,
  melodies in octaves 3–5, harmonics cut off below 3 kHz, a dark reverb. Keep it
  that way: no stacked high registers and almost no energy above 2 kHz.
- Effects: stone clacks as a stroke grows and as a wall is pulled up or down, a
  settling rattle when a wall finishes, a gravel trickle for an arch and a tumble for
  undo, each played from a pool of 3D voices at the stones involved.
- `scripts/render.sh demo/sound.json OUT --write-movie OUT/sound.avi --fixed-fps 30`
  records the game's own mix through an idle stretch, a build and the return.

## Wildlife

Directive (verbatim): "I'd love to get some animals like ducks and stuff, but I... I don't know... how skilled Opus... 5.5... will be at making ducks... and making them cute and pretty"

Wildlife is never placed by the player. Animals show up on their own, as a
surprise, drawn by what you build: dig a pond and, some time later, a duck may
fly in and settle on it. There is no tool, menu or button for animals.

- **Ducks** arrive once a pond exists: one flies in from beyond the meadow,
  glides down and splashes onto the water, then paddles about; now and then one
  waddles up the bank to rest on the grass, and ducklings follow their mother in
  a line. The duck's look is chosen by a side-by-side bake-off of a credited
  CC0 model and one built here in code; a licensed animal, if chosen, is
  credited in a credits file and is the one exception to original assets.
- **A conure**, iridescent blue and green, comes after the ducks: it lands on a
  tree or a wall top. Its plumage is poofy and soft, with a view-dependent hue
  shift (blue facing you, green at grazing angles) computed per fragment from
  the view angle, cheap enough for the headset. It should read as a real bird,
  so its motion (head tilts, preening, hops, flight) comes from a human-animated
  rig, never invented motion.

Budget: each animal is a few hundred triangles, skinned or vertex-animated, one
draw call, and only a handful are ever in view, so wildlife never costs the
refresh rate.

## Verifying on a headset

`scripts/package.sh` builds an aarch64 bundle (the pinned Godot runtime plus the
game); `nix-shell --run 'scripts/deploy.sh BUNDLE HOST'` installs it to `~/cairnfold`
on the headset over SSH, runs Godot's asset import there once, and adds (or
corrects) a "Cairnfold" VR shortcut in the Steam library that runs
`cairnfold/play.sh`. `play.sh --script demo/slice.json
--metrics FILE --capture-dir DIR` plays a scripted session through the same entry
points as the controllers (teleport, float, draw a wall), logs per-second frame
times and saves view captures. `scripts/test.sh` runs the headless layout tests;
`scripts/render.sh` plays a script on a desktop renderer; `demo/flora.json` paints,
plants trees and lets the ivy grow; `demo/houses.json` builds a tower, two houses and
walls in each top style, and shapes the roofs.
